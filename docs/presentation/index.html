An AI assistant that helps field engineers find, understand and verify technical information, built mobile first.

Prototype Figma PRD
Situation

Alex needs the right answer in the field, and the proof behind it

Alex, 34
Field Service Engineer

Installs, inspects and repairs medical equipment in hospitals and clinics, moving between sites.

  • Uses the phone to consult information on site
  • Switches between equipment, manuals and specialist support
  • Must know when the documents are not enough

Design hypothesis, not research

The problem

Manuals, specifications, error codes and troubleshooting guides live in different places. Finding the right one takes time, and the person needs it while standing at the equipment.

Job to be done

"When I need to inspect, maintain or troubleshoot a medical device, I want to find the relevant technical information and understand the next steps, so I can work efficiently while following authorized procedures."

Design decision: mobile first

Alex works in many places and the phone is the fastest tool at hand. I designed for the phone first. The prototype also has a desktop version, a static preview, but the focus is mobile.

Task

Design a copilot people can trust, not just one that answers

FIND

One question, many documents

Searches manuals, specs and procedures from a single query.

INTERPRET

A structured answer

Summary, warnings, steps, for this equipment.

VERIFY

Evidence one tap away

Shows the source, says what it could not substantiate, points to support.

Five risks to design against

  • Unsupported or fabricated answers
  • Outdated documentation
  • Wrong equipment context
  • Overreliance on AI
  • Insufficient evidence

Behind all five: the AI agent must be very well structured, so its research stays coherent with the project and with the user who is searching.

Boundary

The assistant supports the professional. It never authorizes a procedure or replaces manufacturer instructions.

Brief

A responsive, interactive prototype with at least 3 final screens, evaluated on reasoning, UX structure, simplification, visual quality, work with AI and autonomy.

Action 2 · Decisions

Eight decisions, each tied to a risk

DecisionWhy
Tess asks for the firmware instead of assuming itA wrong equipment context gives a wrong procedure
A citation on every step, inside the textVerify without leaving the answer; no separate source list
Warnings come before the stepsA warning below the steps gets missed
Source text and AI explanation in separate blocksNever blend a quote with a paraphrase
"Ruled out" is labelled "not verified by the assistant"Show the limits of what was checked
Insufficient evidence: no steps, escalation firstNever fill a gap with plausible steps
Dark mode only, contrast checkedOne theme fully verified beats two half-verified
No hover, 44 px primary targetsTouch first; pressed state instead of hover

All of these decisions follow the hypotheses, requirements, risks and guardrails defined in the PRD created at the start of the project (sections 4, 8, 9 and 10).

Result

What was delivered, and what is not proven

Delivered

  • 5 final screens in 2 scenarios
  • Design system and developer handoff
  • Testable mobile prototype, live
  • Review against the PRD success criteria, requirements, risks and guardrails

Limits and conscious choices

  • No interviews or usability tests: nothing is claimed about time saved or trust
  • Desktop is a static preview, available in the prototype through the "View desktop version" button; mobile is the tested experience
  • "Simulated content" is flagged outside the phone so the product looks real
  • All manuals and equipment are fictional
